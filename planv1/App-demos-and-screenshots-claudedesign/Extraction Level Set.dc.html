Level Set

A kickoff room full of stakeholders, one shared pile, and a single question: have we defined the universe of what we're being asked to do? Not who is right — what is still dark.

Everything here is a proposal for a hosted, authenticated instance. It reuses the twelve extraction modes unchanged; what changes is who can reach the pile and whether a fragment remembers who said it.

The problem

Kickoffs produce a whiteboard photo and six private mental models. Two weeks later everyone is delivering against a different one.

The move

One pile everybody writes into — in the room and for the week after. The modes tease out what people wouldn't volunteer.

The deliverable

An explicit map of the ask: what is defined, what is partial, what nobody has touched — and the questions and assumptions behind each.

01

A fragment remembers who said it

One change to the pile card

Today a fragment carries a mode and an index. In group mode it also carries an author and their role — stamped from the verified identity, not typed in. That single addition is what makes every other panel on this page possible.

1

Role, not just name. "Business ops lead" is why the fragment matters; the name alone isn't. Roles also drive the coverage map.

2

The mode dot stays. Knowing a fragment came out of a Guided Drill rather than a free dump tells you how much it was pushed on.

3

Nobody edits anyone. Contributions are append-only. A facilitator can merge duplicates, and the survivor keeps every author.

Why it matters for level-setting — attribution turns the pile from a list of notes into evidence of who has and hasn't entered each part of the problem.

06

What this gets a client

A scope you can point at

Ten named areas with a status each. Change requests later get argued against a written boundary instead of a memory of a workshop.

Gaps found in week one

A dark area surfaces on day two rather than in month four. Commercials cost a conversation now; it costs a re-plan later.

Quiet voices on the record

Asynchronous contribution means the people who don't speak up in a room full of executives still shape the scope.

Assumptions made visible

Four things the plan depends on, written down with their source, so nobody discovers them as facts a month in.

One vocabulary

Five words pinned down. "Claim" alone was a three-fold difference in volumes between two stakeholders.

A reusable artefact

Regenerate the same pile as a scope document, a risk register, or a phase-one checklist without running another workshop.

07

How you'd host it

Cloud Run · IAP · Firestore

One hosted instance per engagement. Identity is the client's own, authorisation is IAM, and the app itself barely changes.

Request path

Stakeholder browser 6 + 4 SMEs

Signs in with their existing Workspace account. Client-side and consultant-side users, one instance.

Load balancer + Identity-Aware Proxy Authz here

Access by Google Group membership (roles/iap.httpsResourceAccessor). Unauthorised requests never reach the app; IAP forwards a signed assertion the server verifies.

Cloud Run · the existing Express app Ships today

The same server.ts bundle, plus reading the verified identity and stamping it on each fragment.

Firestore

Replaces localStorage. Sessions keyed by engagement, fragments carry an author. Async contribution falls out of this for free.

Secret Manager → Gemini

The key moves out of .env into Secret Manager, read by the service account. The proxy pattern is already correct.

Why IAP Client thought data is commercially sensitive. No credential handling in the app, revocation through the client's own directory, and Cloud Audit Logs answering "who saw this pile".
